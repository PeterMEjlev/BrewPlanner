# Brülosophy exBEERiments: Clarification, Fining and Conditioning

**Experiments included:** 46

**Source index:** https://brulosophy.com/projects/exbeeriments/

**Prepared:** 29 July 2026

## About this file

This catalogue indexes Brülosophy experiments in which clarification, fining, filtration, trub management, lagering, maturation, vessel residence, natural conditioning, or carbonation method was the tested variable. It is not a copy of the article text. Every experiment includes a direct link to its source article and three sections: purpose, results, and discussion.

**Scope:** Included experiments test kettle finings such as Irish moss and Whirlfloc; hot-break removal, vorlauf, and kettle-trub carryover; gelatin, Biofine Clear, Clarity Ferm, combined fining regimes, and mechanical filtration; lagering, diacetyl rest, extended time in primary or on trub, and transfer to a secondary vessel; and bottle conditioning, keg conditioning, spunding, kräusening, and natural versus force carbonation. Experiments were excluded when clarity or conditioning was only an incidental observation rather than the tested variable.

**Important measurement caution:** Brülosophy normally serves triangle-test samples in opaque cups. The reported p-values therefore test whether tasters could distinguish aroma, flavour, and mouthfeel, not which beer was visually clearer. Visual differences are mentioned only when the source article explicitly reported them, and most were not measured with an instrument.

**Statistical caution:** A significant triangle test supports the conclusion that the tested samples were distinguishable to that panel under those conditions. It does not identify the causal compounds, prove that one method was better, or quantify practical effect size. A non-significant result means that the experiment failed to detect a difference; it is not proof that the treatments were equivalent.

Brülosophy’s earliest experiments did not always use the later standardized triangle-test format. Those entries explicitly describe the comparative or informal nature of the result rather than fabricating a p-value.

The p-value field reproduces the value shown in Brülosophy’s master index. Where an article used an adapted COVID-era test method or the index did not provide a formal value, that limitation is stated in the results section.

### [Impact Irish Moss Hydration Has On An American Light Lager](https://brulosophy.com/2024/05/20/exbeeriment-impact-irish-moss-hydration-has-on-an-american-light-lager/)

**Date:** 2024-05-20  
**Master-index p-value:** 0.85

#### 1. Purpose

Compare an American Light Lager fined in the kettle with Irish moss that had been hydrated before use against the same beer made with the Irish moss added dry.

#### 2. Results

The master index reports p=0.85, so tasters did not reliably distinguish the beer made with hydrated Irish moss from the beer made with dry Irish moss. Both beers were lagered for four weeks before evaluation.

#### 3. Discussion

Pre-hydrating the Irish moss did not create a detectable sensory advantage in this batch. The result does not establish that hydration had no effect on wort sedimentation or measured clarity, because the sensory test was conducted in opaque cups.

### [Impact Fining With Gelatin Has On A Bottle Conditioned Saison](https://brulosophy.com/2026/07/27/exbeeriment-impact-fining-with-gelatin-has-on-a-bottle-conditioned-saison/)

**Date:** 2026-07-27  
**Master-index p-value:** 0.34

#### 1. Purpose

Compare a bottle-conditioned Saison that received gelatin after cold crashing against the same beer bottled without gelatin fining.

#### 2. Results

Twenty tasters participated, and 8 identified the odd sample, below the 11 required for significance (p=0.34). The fined beer was visibly clearer and had less bottle sediment, but carbonated more slowly and remained slightly less fizzy to the brewer.

#### 3. Discussion

Gelatin improved visual clarity without preventing bottle conditioning, although it delayed carbonation in this trial. The non-significant triangle result concerns aroma and flavour in opaque cups, not the obvious appearance difference.
