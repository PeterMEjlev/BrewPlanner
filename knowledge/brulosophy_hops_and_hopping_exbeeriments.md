# Brülosophy exBEERiments: Hops and Hopping

**Experiments included:** 93

**Source index:** https://brulosophy.com/projects/exbeeriments/

## About this file

This catalogue indexes hop-related Brülosophy experiments with concise, original summaries of the tested variable and reported result. It is not a copy of the article text. Every experiment includes a direct link to its source article and three sections: purpose, results, and discussion.

**Scope:** Included experiments test hops, hop products, hop-derived bitterness, hopping technique, hop water, hop and yeast interactions, or processing aids applied specifically around hopping. Experiments conducted in hop-forward beer styles were excluded when the tested variable itself was unrelated to hops.

**Statistical caution:** A significant triangle test supports the conclusion that the tested samples were distinguishable to that panel under those conditions. It does not show which treatment was better or identify the causal flavour compounds. A non-significant result means that the experiment failed to detect a difference; it is not proof that the treatments are equivalent.

Brülosophy’s earliest experiments did not always use the later standardized triangle-test format. Those entries explicitly describe the comparative or informal nature of the result rather than fabricating a p-value.

The p-value field reproduces the value shown in Brülosophy’s master index. Where the underlying article materially disagrees with the index, the discrepancy is stated in the results section.

### [Hop Comparison: LUPOMAX vs. T-90 Cascade Pellets When Holding IBU Constant In A Blonde Ale](https://brulosophy.com/2026/01/12/exbeeriment-hop-comparison-lupomax-vs-t-90-cascade-pellets-when-holding-ibu-constant-in-a-blonde-ale/)

**Date:** 2026-01-12  
**Master-index p-value:** 0.52

#### 1. Purpose

Compare a Blonde Ale bittered with Cascade LUPOMAX against one using a larger charge of Cascade T-90 pellets, with predicted IBU held constant.

#### 2. Results

Twenty tasters participated, and 7 correctly identified the odd sample, below the 11 required for significance (p=0.52). The brewer was correct in 3 of 5 attempts and considered the beers identical. Laboratory testing measured 25 IBU in the LUPOMAX beer and 21.5 IBU in the T-90 beer.

#### 3. Discussion

Using substantially less LUPOMAX produced a beer the panel could not reliably distinguish from the T-90 version, despite a modest measured IBU difference. This supports reduced usage rates for this product in this recipe, but does not establish identical aroma extraction or performance for other varieties and additions.

### [Hop Comparison: Northern Brewer vs. Target In An American Pale Ale](https://brulosophy.com/2023/12/11/exbeeriment-hop-comparison-northern-brewer-vs-target-in-an-american-pale-ale/)

**Date:** 2023-12-11  
**Master-index p-value:** 0.038

#### 1. Purpose

Compare an American Pale Ale hopped entirely with Northern Brewer against the same beer hopped entirely with Target.

#### 2. Results

Of 20 tasters, exactly 11 identified the odd sample, meeting the significance threshold (p=0.038). Seven correct tasters preferred Northern Brewer, 3 preferred Target, and 1 had no preference. The brewer associated the Northern Brewer beer with a slight mint note.

#### 3. Discussion

The closely related varieties were distinguishable in this single-hop recipe, so parentage alone did not make them sensory substitutes. Preference was not unanimous, and the result does not rank either hop as generally superior.

### [Impact Storing Hops In A Non-Purged Baggie Has On An American Pale Ale](https://brulosophy.com/2017/07/17/hop-storage-vacuum-sealed-vs-non-purged-baggie-exbeeriment-results/)

**Date:** 2017-07-17  
**Master-index p-value:** 0.83

#### 1. Purpose

Compare beer made with Motueka hops stored frozen for six months in a non-purged zipper bag against beer made with vacuum-sealed hops from the same lot.

#### 2. Results

Sixteen tasters participated, and only 4 identified the odd sample, below the 9 required for significance (p=0.83). The brewer could distinguish the beers more consistently and perceived a faint freezer-like character in the poorly sealed hops beer.

#### 3. Discussion

The panel did not reliably detect a finished-beer effect from this storage difference, despite obvious differences when smelling the stored hops. The brewer's contrasting experience and the single hop lot make this limited evidence, not a case against oxygen-limited hop storage.

## General limitations

Most exBEERiments are single brewing comparisons with modest sensory panels. Hop lot, crop year, storage history, dosage, contact time, wort composition, yeast, water chemistry, oxygen exposure, equipment, panel composition, statistical power, and evaluation timing can all affect the outcome. These summaries should therefore be used to guide brewing decisions and follow-up tests, not as universal brewing laws.
