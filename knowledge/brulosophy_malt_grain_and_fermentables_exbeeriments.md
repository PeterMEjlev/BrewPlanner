## About this file

This catalogue indexes Brülosophy experiments in which malt, grain, cereal adjuncts, sugars, syrups, honey, malt extract, or grain-specific preparation was the tested variable. It contains concise, original paraphrases rather than copies of the article text. Every experiment includes a link to its source article and separate purpose, results, and discussion sections.

**Scope:** Included experiments directly test base malt, specialty malt, roasted or smoked grain, cereal adjuncts, fermentable sugars and syrups, honey, cider or seltzer fermentable bases, malt extract, or grain-specific handling such as crush, ageing, toasting, decoction placement, and roasted-grain extraction. General mash-temperature, mash-length, sparging, boil, water, yeast, fruit, spice, coffee, and wood experiments were excluded unless the tested variable specifically concerned the malt, grain, or fermentable itself.

**Statistical caution:** A significant triangle test supports the conclusion that the tested samples were distinguishable to that panel under those conditions. It does not show which treatment was better or identify the causal flavour compounds. A non-significant result means that the experiment failed to detect a difference; it is not proof that the treatments are equivalent.

Brülosophy’s earliest experiments did not always use the later standardized triangle-test format. Those entries describe the result as informal or comparative rather than fabricating a p-value.

The p-value field reproduces the value shown in Brülosophy’s master index. Result summaries state what that index value supports statistically; readers should consult the linked article for panel composition, recipe details, brewer impressions, and article-specific caveats.

## Base malts and regional malt comparisons

### [Grain Comparison: German vs. Czech Vienna Malt In A Czech Premium Pale Lager](https://brulosophy.com/2025/12/29/exbeeriment-grain-comparison-german-vs-czech-vienna-malt-in-a-czech-premium-pale-lager/)

**Date:** 2025-12-29  
**Master-index p-value:** 0.99

#### 1. Purpose

Compare a Czech Premium Pale Lager made entirely with German Vienna malt against an otherwise matched version made entirely with Czech Vienna malt.

#### 2. Results

Twenty-one tasters participated, and only 3 correctly identified the odd sample, well below the 12 required for significance (p=0.99). The German-malt beer finished at 1.012 and the Czech-malt beer at 1.013. The brewer identified the odd sample in 5 of 7 semi-blind trials and perceived the Czech-malt beer as fuller and more characterful.

#### 3. Discussion

The panel did not detect a reliable difference between the base-malt treatments in this beer. This supports practical substitution under these conditions, but it is not proof that the malts are chemically identical or interchangeable in every style and process.
