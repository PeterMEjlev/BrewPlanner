**Experiments included:** 79

**Source index:** https://brulosophy.com/projects/exbeeriments/

## About this file

This catalogue contains concise, original paraphrases of Brülosophy experiments in which boiling, chilling, hot-side additions, kettle clarification, wort transfer, oxygen exposure, or pre-pitch wort handling was the tested variable. It is not a copy of the article text. Every experiment includes a direct source link and separate purpose, results, and discussion sections.

**Scope:** Included experiments test boil duration, vigor, coverage, or omission; wort concentration, dilution, pH, and acidification around the boil; ingredient timing in the kettle; hot break, kettle trub, finings, and hop filtration; rapid chilling, no-chill methods, and chiller type; kettle hopping and post-boil hop stands; hot-side aeration; wort oxygenation before fermentation; and delayed yeast pitching.

**Exclusions:** General mash and lautering trials, cold-side oxidation and packaging experiments, fermentation-vessel additions unrelated to a direct hot-side comparison, and ingredient comparisons where boil or wort handling was not the manipulated variable are outside this catalogue.

**Cross-listing:** Some entries also appear in the Hops and Hopping, Malt, Grain and Fermentables, Mash and Lautering, or Yeast and Fermentation catalogues because the tested variable spans more than one brewing stage.

**Statistical caution:** A significant triangle test supports the conclusion that the tested samples were distinguishable to that panel under those conditions. It does not show which treatment was better or identify the causal flavour compounds. A non-significant result means that the experiment failed to detect a difference; it is not proof that the treatments are equivalent.

Brülosophy’s earliest experiments did not always use the later standardized triangle-test format. Those entries explicitly describe the comparative or informal method used instead of fabricating a p-value.

The p-value field reproduces Brülosophy’s master-index value. Where the master index omits a value or differs from the linked article, the result summary states the article-level figure separately.

## Boil duration, vigor, coverage, and no-boil methods

### [Stacked Variables: Impact Reduced Mash & Boil Times Have On An American Stout](https://brulosophy.com/2023/05/22/exbeeriment-stacked-variables-impact-reduced-mash-boil-times-have-on-an-american-stout/)

**Date:** 2023-05-22  
**Master-index p-value:** 0.25

#### 1. Purpose

Compare an American Stout made with 30-minute mash and boil steps against the same recipe made with conventional 60-minute mash and boil steps.

#### 2. Results

The beers finished at 1.011 and 1.012. Ten of 24 tasters identified the odd sample, below the significance requirement (p=0.25), and the brewer was correct in only 2 of 5 semi-blind trials.

#### 3. Discussion

Halving both process steps did not create a reliably detectable difference in this stout. Because mash and boil duration changed together, the experiment cannot show whether either variable alone was neutral.

### [The Boil Effect: Lid On vs Lid Off In A Munich Helles](https://brulosophy.com/2021/05/10/the-boil-effect-lid-on-vs-lid-off-in-a-munich-helles-exbeeriment-results/)

**Date:** 2021-05-10  
**Master-index p-value:** 0.21

#### 1. Purpose

Compare a Munich Helles boiled with the kettle lid in place against the same wort boiled uncovered.

#### 2. Results

In 10 semi-blind triangle tests, the brewer correctly identified the odd beer 5 times, below the 7 required for significance (p=0.21). The beers were regarded as sensorially alike, with no reliable dimethyl sulfide distinction.

#### 3. Discussion

Covering the kettle did not create a detectable difference in this trial. Evaporation rate and volatile removal depend on kettle geometry, heat input, lid fit, malt, and chilling speed, so this does not establish that every covered boil is risk-free.

### [No-Boil Effect: Evaluating The Impact On New England IPA](https://brulosophy.com/2021/04/05/the-no-boil-effect-evaluating-the-impact-on-new-england-ipa-exbeeriment-results/)

**Date:** 2021-04-05  
**Master-index p-value:** 0.21

#### 1. Purpose

Compare a New England IPA made with a conventional boil against the same recipe heated only enough to complete the hot-side process without boiling.

#### 2. Results

The no-boil beer was visibly lighter. In 10 semi-blind triangle tests, the brewer selected the odd sample 5 times, which was not significant (p=0.21), and described the beers as sensorially very similar.

#### 3. Discussion

Skipping the boil did not produce a reliable flavour difference for this heavily hopped beer in the single-person trial. The method still changes sanitation margin, evaporation, protein coagulation, bitterness development, and volatile removal.

### [Boil Vigor: Weak vs. Strong In A German Helles Exportbier](https://brulosophy.com/2021/02/15/boil-vigor-weak-vs-strong-in-a-german-helles-exportbier-exbeeriment-results/)

**Date:** 2021-02-15  
**Master-index p-value:** 0.441

#### 1. Purpose

Compare a German Helles Exportbier produced with a weak simmer against the same recipe subjected to a vigorous rolling boil.

#### 2. Results

The weak-boil wort reached 1.053 original gravity and the vigorous-boil wort 1.059; the beers finished at 1.009 and 1.010. The brewer was correct in 4 of 10 semi-blind tests (p=0.441).

#### 3. Discussion

Boil intensity altered concentration but did not produce a reliably detectable sensory difference after the batches were otherwise handled alike. The result does not show that boil vigor is irrelevant to evaporation control, hop utilisation, or equipment sizing.

### [No-Boil Effect: Assessing The Impact On A Scottish Heavy](https://brulosophy.com/2018/07/02/the-no-boil-effect-exbeeriment-results/)

**Date:** 2018-07-02  
**Master-index p-value:** 0.021

#### 1. Purpose

Compare a Scottish Heavy made with a conventional boil against the same recipe made without boiling the wort.

#### 2. Results

Of 21 tasters, 12 identified the odd sample, exactly meeting the significance requirement (p=0.021). Eight correct tasters preferred the boiled beer, two preferred the no-boil beer, one had no preference, and one reported no perceived difference.

#### 3. Discussion

The boil produced a detectable effect in this low-gravity, malt-focused beer. Preference leaned toward the boiled version, but the experiment does not determine whether the cause was wort concentration, Maillard chemistry, volatile removal, bitterness, or hot-break formation.
