## About this file

This catalogue contains concise, original paraphrases of the individual Brülosophy articles. It is not a copy of the article text. Every experiment includes a direct link to its source article and three sections: purpose, results, and discussion.

**Statistical caution:** A significant triangle test supports the conclusion that the tested samples were distinguishable to that panel under those conditions. It does not show which treatment was better or identify the causal flavour compounds. A non-significant result means that the experiment failed to detect a difference; it is not proof that the treatments are equivalent.

Brülosophy’s earliest experiments did not always use the later standardized triangle-test format. Those entries explicitly describe the comparative survey or informal method used instead of fabricating a p-value.

## Yeast strains and culture comparisons

### [Yeast Comparison: White Labs WLP001 California Ale vs. Safale US-05 American Ale In A Session IPA](https://brulosophy.com/2026/06/29/exbeeriment-yeast-comparison-white-labs-wlp001-california-ale-vs-safale-us-05-american-ale-in-a-session-ipa/)

**Date:** 2026-06-29  
**Master-index p-value:** 0.00000001

#### 1. Purpose

Compare a Session IPA fermented with White Labs WLP001 California Ale against the same wort fermented with Safale US-05 American Ale.

#### 2. Results

Eighty-four tasters participated, and 52 correctly identified the odd sample, producing a highly significant result (p=0.00000001). Among the correct tasters, 31 preferred the US-05 beer, 12 preferred WLP001, 7 had no preference, and 2 reported no perceived difference.

#### 3. Discussion

The two closely related American ale cultures produced beers that were reliably distinguishable in this recipe. The panel preference favoured US-05, but this is not evidence that US-05 is generally superior; strain handling, laboratory history, attenuation, and interaction with the specific wort may all have contributed.

### [Yeast Comparison: Imperial Yeast L13 Global vs. Imperial Yeast A07 Flagship In A West Coast IPA](https://brulosophy.com/2024/11/18/exbeeriment-yeast-comparison-imperial-yeast-l13-global-vs-imperial-yeast-a07-flagship-in-a-west-coast-ipa/)

**Date:** 2024-11-18  
**Master-index p-value:** 0.52

#### 1. Purpose

Compare a West Coast IPA fermented at 60°F/16°C with lager strain Imperial Yeast L13 Global against the same wort fermented with ale strain Imperial Yeast A07 Flagship.

#### 2. Results

The beers finished at 1.007 and 1.006. Of 26 tasters, 9 correctly identified the odd sample, below the 14 required for significance (p=0.52). The brewer could identify a subtle difference in four of five attempts, describing A07 as slightly sharper and L13 as cleaner and clearer, and preferred the L13 beer.

#### 3. Discussion

The panel did not reliably distinguish the lager- and ale-fermented IPAs under these conditions. Strong hop character may have obscured small fermentation differences, and both cultures are relatively clean. The result supports L13 Global as a workable option for a West Coast IPA, but does not show that ale and lager strains are generally interchangeable.

### [Impact Yeast Has On Hop Water](https://brulosophy.com/2023/07/17/exbeeriment-impact-yeast-has-on-hop-water/)

**Date:** 2023-07-17  
**Master-index p-value:** 0.0000002

#### 1. Purpose

Compare hop water dosed with Imperial Yeast A07 Flagship with an otherwise identical hop water receiving no yeast.

#### 2. Results

Of 20 tasters, 18 correctly identified the odd sample, far exceeding the 11 required for significance (p=0.0000002). The brewer found the yeast-treated version bright, crisp, citrusy, and refreshing, while the untreated version was sharp, stale, and reminiscent of oxidised IPA.

#### 3. Discussion

Adding yeast had a large sensory effect despite the absence of a conventional fermentable wort. Biotransformation is a plausible explanation, but yeast may also have scavenged oxygen or altered other compounds. The experiment demonstrates a difference, not the precise biochemical mechanism.

### [Impact Fermenting With Thiolized Yeast Has On A Mash Hopped International Pale Lager - The Brü Club xBmt](https://brulosophy.com/2023/02/24/the-bru-club-xbmt-series-impact-fermenting-with-thiolized-yeast-has-on-a-mash-hopped-international-pale-lager/)

**Date:** 2023-02-23  
**Master-index p-value:** 0.00000008

#### 1. Purpose

Compare a mash-hopped International Pale Lager fermented with thiolised Omega OYL-403 Lunar Crush against the same beer fermented with its non-thiolised parent strain, OYL-113 Mexican Lager.

#### 2. Results

Both beers finished at 1.020. Of 21 tasters, 19 correctly identified the odd sample, producing a highly significant result (p=0.00000008). The brewer repeatedly identified the thiolised version by its pronounced orange and guava aroma, while bitterness, sweetness, and mouthfeel were similar.

#### 3. Discussion

The thiolised strain produced a clear sensory change in a simple lager wort containing a large Saaz mash-hop addition. The recipe and lack of dry hopping likely made the released thiol character easier to perceive. This result should not be assumed to apply when precursor-rich ingredients are absent or intense dry-hop character dominates.

### [Yeast Comparison: Omega Yeast OYL-071 Lutra Kveik vs. Saflager W-34/70 In A German Pils - The Brü Club xBmt](https://brulosophy.com/2022/03/03/the-bru-club-xbmt-series-yeast-comparison-omega-yeast-oyl-071-lutra-kveik-vs-saflager-w-34-70-in-a-german-pils/)

#### 1. Purpose

Compare a German Pils fermented at 66°F/19°C with Omega OYL-071 Lutra Kveik against the same wort fermented at 52°F/11°C with Saflager W-34/70.

### [Yeast Comparison: LalBrew New England vs. Safale S-04 English Ale - The Brü Club xBmt](https://brulosophy.com/2020/09/24/yeast-comparison-lalbrew-new-england-vs-safale-s-04-english-ale-the-bru-club-xbmt-series/)

**Date:** 2020-09-24  
**Master-index p-value:** 0.042

#### 1. Purpose

Evaluate whether the same hazy IPA fermented with LalBrew New England yeast or Safale S-04 English Ale yeast would differ perceptibly.

#### 2. Results

Pandemic restrictions prevented the usual public panel. The author instead performed repeated semi-blind triangle tests and was able to distinguish the beers reliably enough for the index to report p=0.042. He perceived the LalBrew New England beer as more tropical and juicy, with peach and mango alongside citrus and pine, while the S-04 beer seemed cleaner, more lemon-forward, slightly less hop-saturated, and carried a subtle characteristic tang. He preferred the LalBrew New England beer, whereas his wife preferred the S-04 version.

#### 3. Discussion

The trial suggests these dry strains were not sensory substitutes in this recipe, despite both being plausible choices for hazy IPA. The author attributed the distinction mainly to hop expression rather than gross fermentation defects. Because the evidence came from one brewer's repeated tests rather than the standard independent panel, it is weaker and less generalisable than a normal Brülosophy result.

### [Yeast Comparison: Fleischmann’s Active Dry Yeast vs. Safale US-05 American Ale Yeast](https://brulosophy.com/2020/03/30/yeast-comparison-fleischmanns-active-dry-yeast-vs-safale-us-05-american-ale-yeast-exbeeriment-results/)

Determine whether an American pale ale fermented with Fleischmann's baker's yeast would differ from the same beer fermented with Safale US-05 brewing yeast.

#### 2. Results

The baker's-yeast beer finished at 1.009, while the US-05 beer finished at 1.015. Of 20 tasters, 12 correctly identified the odd sample, exceeding the 11 required for significance (p=0.013). ### [Imperial Yeast A09 Pub vs. Lallemand London ESB - The Brü Club xBmt](https://brulosophy.com/2020/01/30/yeast-comparison-imperial-yeast-a09-pub-vs-lallemand-london-esb-the-bru-club-xbmt-series/)

#### 1. Purpose

Compare an American brown ale fermented with Imperial Yeast A09 Pub against the same wort fermented with Lallemand London ESB.

#### 2. Results

The A09 Pub beer finished at 1.013 and the London ESB beer at 1.020. Seventeen tasters participated; eight correctly identified the odd sample, below the 10 required for significance (p=0.17). ### [Yeast Comparison: Wyeast 1318 London Ale III vs. Safale S-04 English Ale - The Brü Club xBmt](https://brulosophy.com/2019/05/30/yeast-comparison-wyeast-1318-london-ale-iii-vs-safale-s-04-english-ale-the-bru-club-xbmt-series/)

**Master-index p-value:** 0.46

#### 1. Purpose

Compare a hazy IPA fermented with Wyeast 1318 London Ale III against the same wort fermented with Safale S-04 English Ale.

#### 2. Results

Both beers finished at 1.012. Twenty-two tasters participated, and eight correctly identified the odd sample, below the 12 required for significance (p=0.46). At kegging, the author thought the 1318 beer was brighter, juicier, more citrus-forward, and hazier, while S-04 seemed slightly muted and carried a bready or doughy tang. Compare a German Pils fermented with White Labs WLP833 German Bock yeast against the same wort fermented with WLP838 Southern German Lager yeast.

### [Yeast Comparison: Safale US-05 vs. Saflager W-34/70 In An American Pale Ale](https://brulosophy.com/2017/03/09/yeast-comparison-safale-us-05-vs-saflager-w-3470-exbeeriment-results/)

Compare an American pale ale fermented at 66°F/19°C with Safale US-05 ale yeast against the same wort fermented with Saflager W-34/70 lager yeast.

### [Yeast Comparison: Saflager W-34/70 vs. Saflager S-23 In A Munich Helles](https://brulosophy.com/2017/01/19/yeast-comparison-saflager-w-3470-vs-saflager-s-23-exbeeriment-results/)

**Master-index p-value:** 0.39

#### 1. Purpose

Evaluate whether a Munich Helles fermented with Saflager W-34/70 differs perceptibly from the same wort fermented with Saflager S-23.

#### 2. Results

Both beers reached the same final gravity. Eighteen tasters participated; seven correctly identified the odd sample, below the 10 required for significance (p=0.39). ### [Yeast Comparison: WLP400 Belgian Wit I vs. WLP410 Belgian Wit II In A Witbier](https://brulosophy.com/2015/11/16/yeast-comparison-wlp400-belgian-wit-ale-vs-wlp410-belgian-wit-ii-ale/)

#### 1. Purpose

Compare a Witbier fermented with White Labs WLP400 Belgian Wit Ale against the same wort fermented with the limited-release WLP410 Belgian Wit II.

Four correct tasters preferred the promptly packaged beer, 2 preferred the extended-primary beer, 5 had no preference, and 2 later said they perceived no difference. The brewer was correct in 3 of 5 trials and described any difference as subtle, with a possible older or oxidized character.

#### 3. Discussion

The article concludes that the three-week delay had a perceptible effect, but explicitly states that autolysis was not demonstrated. The beers were also held at somewhat different temperatures during the delay, creating a possible confound. The result therefore supports a timing-related difference in this execution, not a general claim that three weeks on yeast damages beer.

### [Impact Of Delaying Yeast Pitch On A Hazy IPA](https://brulosophy.com/2019/01/07/the-impact-of-delaying-yeast-pitch-exbeeriment-results/)

**Date:** 2019-01-07  
**Master-index p-value:** 0.88

#### 1. Purpose

Compare a Hazy IPA pitched shortly after chilling against the same wort held for 40 hours before yeast was added.

#### 2. Results

Both batches showed activity within hours of their respective pitches and fermented similarly. Of 21 tasters, only 5 correctly identified the odd sample, below the 12 required for significance (p=0.88). The brewer also failed to distinguish them and described the beers as tasting the same.

#### 3. Discussion

No contamination or detectable sensory penalty appeared from the 40-hour delay in this trial. The article nevertheless treats delayed pitching as an unnecessary exposure to microbial risk rather than a useful technique. A clean result from one well-sanitized batch does not make prolonged unpitched wort broadly safe.

### [Sugar Additions: Boil vs. High Kräusen In A Belgian Golden Strong](https://brulosophy.com/2017/12/25/sugar-additions-boil-vs-high-krausen-exbeeriment-results/)

**Date:** 2017-12-25  
**Master-index p-value:** 0.12

#### 1. Purpose

Compare a Belgian Golden Strong Ale with candi syrup added during the boil against the same beer receiving the syrup at high kräusen two days into fermentation.

#### 2. Results

Both beers finished at the same 1.008 final gravity. Of 26 tasters, 12 correctly identified the odd sample, below the 14 needed for significance (p=0.12). The brewer chose correctly in 3 of 5 trials and found both beers nearly identical, with the same spicy aroma, low alcohol notes, and grape-like flavour.

#### 3. Discussion

The article found no support for claims that adding the syrup at high kräusen improves attenuation, protects delicate sugar character, or produces a cleaner beer under these conditions. Together with the earlier staggered-sugar test, it suggests timing may often matter less than expected when fermentation is healthy. It does not test higher osmotic stress, another strain, or an actually problematic fermentation.

### [Sugar Additions: Single Dose vs. Staggered During Fermentation In A Belgian Golden Strong Ale](https://brulosophy.com/2016/01/25/sugar-during-fermentation-single-dose-vs-staggered-additions-exbeeriment-results/)

**Date:** 2016-01-25  
**Master-index p-value:** 0.10

#### 1. Purpose

Compare two Belgian Golden Strong Ales where one received all sugar at the beginning of fermentation and the other received the same amount in three daily additions.

#### 2. Results

The single-dose beer started slightly faster and finished at 1.004, versus 1.005 for the staggered beer. Of 37 tasters, 16 correctly identified the odd sample, below the 18 required for significance (p=0.10). Seven correct tasters preferred the staggered beer, 6 preferred the single-dose beer, and 3 had no preference. The brewer initially performed near chance but later found the single-dose beer slightly more estery, sweet, and warming as it matured and warmed in the glass.

#### 3. Discussion

The article acknowledges that staggered additions may act as insurance against stalled fermentation, but found little objective or sensory difference here. Because one addition is simpler than three, the brewer leaned toward the single-dose method for similar beers. The near-threshold result and later personal discrimination justify caution rather than a claim of equivalence.

### [Impact Racking To A Secondary Fermenter Has On An American Pale Ale](https://brulosophy.com/2014/08/12/primary-only-vs-transfer-to-secondary-exbeeriment-results/)

**Date:** 2014-08-12  
**Master-index p-value:** N/A

#### 1. Purpose

Compare an American Pale Ale left in its original fermenter through conditioning and dry hopping against the same beer transferred to a secondary vessel after primary fermentation. The early study focused on perceived aroma, flavour, mouthfeel, clarity, and preference rather than a formal triangle test.

#### 2. Results

Sixteen blind participants completed the comparative survey. Fifteen of 16 rated the beers' overall aroma as somewhat similar and the panel reported little separation in flavour or mouthfeel. Only one person considered the secondary beer clearer, while the large majority saw no clarity difference. After formal data collection, uninformed visitors served the beers sequentially commonly failed to realize they had received two different treatments. No formal p-value was calculated.

#### 3. Discussion

The article concluded that routine transfer to secondary did not improve this quick-turnaround Pale Ale and added time, cleaning, and oxidation or contamination risk. It did not argue that secondary vessels are never useful, specifically preserving possible roles for long bulk ageing, lagering, sour beer, fruit, wood, or other additions. Because the study predates Brülosophy's standardized triangle-test method, its evidence is more descriptive than the later entries.

### [Fermentation Vessel: HDPE Bucket vs. PET Carboy In A Pale Ale](https://brulosophy.com/2020/11/23/fermentation-vessel-hdpe-bucket-vs-pet-carboy-in-a-pale-ale-exbeeriment-results/)

**Date:** 2020-11-23  
**Master-index p-value:** 0.21

#### 1. Purpose

Compare a Pale Ale fermented in a food-grade HDPE bucket against the same wort fermented in a PET carboy.

#### 2. Results

Both beers reached the same original and final gravities, finishing at 1.005. Under pandemic restrictions, the brewer completed ten semi-blind triangle tests and identified the odd sample 5 times, below the 7 required for significance (p=0.21). Differences he thought he noticed at packaging had disappeared after conditioning, and he described the beers as identical.

The article found no detectable penalty from using the inexpensive bucket in this short fermentation. ### [Fermentation Vessel: Stainless Ss Brew Bucket vs. Plastic Speidel Tank In An American Pale Ale](https://brulosophy.com/2017/10/23/fermentation-vessel-pt-5-stainless-ss-brew-bucket-vs-plastic-speidel-tank-exbeeriment-results/)

### [Fermentation Vessel: Corny Keg vs. Glass Carboy In An American Amber Ale](https://brulosophy.com/2017/02/13/fermentation-vessel-pt-4-corny-keg-vs-glass-carboy-exbeeriment-results/)

Compare an American Amber Ale fermented in a tall stainless Cornelius keg against the same beer fermented in a wider glass carboy, while using pressure transfer for both to reduce packaging differences.

#### 2. Results

The keg beer finished at 1.012 and the carboy beer at 1.013. Of 29 tasters, 16 correctly identified the odd sample, exceeding the 15 required (article p=0.013; master index rounded to 0.01). Six preferred the carboy beer, 3 preferred the keg beer, 3 had no preference, and 4 later reported no perceived difference. The brewer himself identified only 1 of 3 trials and considered the beers too similar to distinguish.

#### 3. Discussion

The article regarded the significant panel result as evidence that vessel shape, surface area, yeast contact, headspace, light exposure, or another correlated factor may influence beer. It did not identify which factor was responsible. Preference leaned toward glass, but the brewer emphasized that this did not make keg fermentation objectively worse and continued using kegs for their process advantages.

### [Fermentation Vessel: Stainless Steel vs. Glass Carboy In An American IPA](https://brulosophy.com/2016/07/11/fermentation-vessel-pt-3-stainless-steel-vs-glass-carboy-exbeeriment-results/)

### [Fermentation Vessel: Plastic (PET) Carboy vs. Glass Carboy In A California Common](https://brulosophy.com/2016/05/16/fermentor-type-pt-2-plastic-pet-vs-glass-carboy-exbeeriment-results/)

Compare a California Common fermented in a PET carboy against the same wort fermented in a glass carboy.

#### 2. Results

### [Fermentation Vessel: Plastic (PET) Carboy vs. Bucket In An American IPA](https://brulosophy.com/2014/10/14/the-impact-of-fermentation-vessel-exbeeriment-results/)

**Date:** 2014-10-14  
**Master-index p-value:** 0.007

#### 1. Purpose

Compare an American IPA produced from one wort and one yeast pitch, split between a PET carboy and a plastic bucket as the only planned fermentation variable.

#### 2. Results

Ten blind tasters participated in the early triangle test. Seven correctly identified the odd sample (p=0.007). Most correct tasters considered the beers similar in appearance and mouthfeel, but 4 of 7 preferred the carboy-fermented beer; only one reported no detectable difference. After reviewing the results, the brewer also perceived a subtle difference despite not expecting one.

#### 3. Discussion

The article treated the finding as surprising evidence that vessel choice might affect beer, while warning against selecting equipment from this small trial alone. Potential causes such as oxygen permeability, geometry, headspace, or the condition and history of the bucket were not isolated. The small panel makes this an important prompt for replication rather than a definitive ranking of PET and bucket fermentation.

## General limitations

Most exBEERiments are single brewing comparisons with modest sensory panels. Recipe, yeast health, equipment, temperature control, panel composition, statistical power, and evaluation timing can all affect the outcome. These summaries should therefore be used to generate brewing decisions and follow-up tests, not as universal brewing laws.
