# Brülosophy exBEERiments: Storage, Ageing and Transport

**Experiments included:** 40

**Source index:** https://brulosophy.com/projects/exbeeriments/

**Prepared:** 29 July 2026

## About this file

This catalogue indexes Brülosophy experiments pertaining to beer and ingredient storage, ageing, maturation, preservation, and transport with concise, original summaries of the tested variable and reported result. It is not a copy of the article text. Every experiment includes a direct link to its source article and three sections: purpose, results, and discussion.

**Scope:** Included experiments directly test storage temperature, storage duration, light exposure, freezing, storage vessels, simulated shipping, deliberate maturation time, extended vessel residence, ingredient age or storage, yeast preservation, or the handling of aged yeast. Follow-up experiments explicitly designed to examine how a previously imposed treatment developed during ageing are also included. Experiments that merely happened to include routine cold conditioning, lagering, or stored ingredients were excluded when those factors were not part of the tested variable.

**Overlap with other catalogues:** Storage and ageing often interact with oxidation, packaging, clarification, hops, and yeast. Relevant experiments are retained here even when they also fit one of those categories.

**Transport coverage:** Brülosophy’s master index contains one experiment explicitly simulating warm shipping. No indexed exBEERiment was found that isolates vibration, shaking, or other mechanical transport stress.

**Statistical caution:** A significant triangle test supports the conclusion that the tested samples were distinguishable to that panel under those conditions. It does not show which treatment was better or identify the causal flavour compounds. A non-significant result means that the experiment failed to detect a difference; it is not proof that the treatments are equivalent.

Brülosophy’s earliest experiments and several experiments conducted during pandemic restrictions did not always use the later standardized panel format. Those entries describe the actual comparative or brewer-run method rather than presenting it as a conventional panel test.

The p-value field reproduces the value shown in Brülosophy’s master index. Where the underlying article reports a more precise value or materially differs from the index, the article-level value is noted in the results section.

## Finished-beer storage temperature, light, freezing and age

### [Impact Storage Temperature Has On Bottled West Coast Pils](https://brulosophy.com/exbeeriments/exbeeriment-impact-storage-temperature-has-on-bottled-west-coast-pils/)

**Date:** 2024-09-23  
**Master-index p-value:** 0.0002

#### 1. Purpose

Compare bottled West Coast Pils stored warm against bottles of the same beer kept cold.

#### 2. Results

The master index reports p=0.0002, indicating the panel reliably distinguished the warm-stored and cold-stored bottles under the conditions of this experiment.

#### 3. Discussion

Storage temperature produced a detectable effect in this hop-forward lager. The result supports refrigerated storage for preserving this beer, but it does not isolate whether the difference arose from hop degradation, oxidation, carbonation changes, or another temperature-dependent reaction.

### [Impact Sunlight Exposure In Amber Bottles Has On An American Blonde Ale](https://brulosophy.com/2023/01/23/exbeeriment-impact-sunlight-exposure-in-amber-bottles-has-on-an-american-blonde-ale/)

**Date:** 2023-01-23  
**Master-index p-value:** 0.90

#### 1. Purpose

Evaluate whether exposing an American Blonde Ale in amber bottles to 30 minutes of direct sunlight produces a perceptible difference from bottles kept dark.

#### 2. Results

Twenty-two tasters participated, and only 5 identified the odd sample, below the 12 required for significance (p=0.90). The brewer also failed to identify the light-exposed beer consistently and reported no obvious lightstruck character.

#### 3. Discussion

Amber glass prevented a reliably detectable effect from this particular exposure. This does not mean amber bottles provide complete protection under longer or more intense exposure, or that clear and green bottles would perform similarly.

### [Impact Storage Temperature Has On A Canned Hazy IPA](https://brulosophy.com/2023/01/16/exbeeriment-impact-storage-temperature-has-on-a-canned-hazy-ipa/)

**Date:** 2023-01-16  
**Master-index p-value:** 0.02

#### 1. Purpose

Compare canned Hazy IPA stored for 8 weeks at 38°F/3°C against cans stored at 68°F/20°C.

#### 2. Results

Of 21 tasters, exactly 12 identified the odd sample, meeting the significance threshold; the article reports p=0.021 while the master index rounds this to 0.02. Four correct tasters preferred the cold-stored beer, 6 preferred the warm-stored beer, 1 had no preference, and 1 reported no difference.

#### 3. Discussion

Eight weeks of warm storage produced a detectable difference, but preference did not favour refrigeration in this panel. The trial establishes sensory change, not that the warm-stored beer was objectively worse or which chemical changes caused the difference.

### [Impact Storage Temperature Has On An Oxygen-Exposed Hazy IPA](https://brulosophy.com/2022/05/02/exbeeriment-impact-storage-temperature-has-on-an-oxygen-exposed-hazy-ipa/)

**Date:** 2022-05-02  
**Master-index p-value:** < 0.0001

#### 1. Purpose

Determine whether warm storage accelerates perceptible deterioration in Hazy IPA deliberately exposed to oxygen during kegging.

#### 2. Results

Eighteen of 24 tasters identified the odd sample after the beers spent 10 days at either 38°F/3°C or about 70°F/21°C; the article reports p=0.000007. Eleven correct tasters preferred the cool-stored beer, 2 preferred the warm-stored beer, and 5 had no preference.

#### 3. Discussion

Warm storage strongly amplified the sensory consequences of oxygen exposure in this beer. Because both oxygen exposure and storage temperature were severe and style-specific, the magnitude should not be generalized to well-packaged beer or less oxidation-sensitive styles.

### [Impact Storage Temperature Has On A Canned Kölsch](https://brulosophy.com/2022/02/14/exbeeriment-impact-storage-temperature-has-on-a-canned-kolsch/)

**Date:** 2022-02-14  
**Master-index p-value:** 0.12

#### 1. Purpose

Compare canned Kölsch stored for 8 weeks at 34°F/1°C against cans stored at 78°F/26°C.

#### 2. Results

Twenty-one tasters participated, and 10 identified the odd sample, below the 12 required for significance (p=0.12). The brewer perceived the warm-stored version as slightly more carbonated but did not report a large flavour difference.

#### 3. Discussion

This panel did not reliably detect an 8-week storage-temperature effect despite a large temperature difference. The result contrasts with several significant lager and IPA trials and shows that package, style, oxygen level, and execution can materially affect storage outcomes.

### [Impact Storage Temperature Has On Cream Ale](https://brulosophy.com/2021/07/26/impact-storage-temperature-has-on-cream-ale-exbeeriment-results/)

**Date:** 2021-07-26  
**Master-index p-value:** 0.70

#### 1. Purpose

Compare Cream Ale kept cold against the same beer stored for 3 days in a garage ranging from 70°F/21°C to 90°F/32°C.

#### 2. Results

The brewer completed 10 semi-blind triangle tests and identified the odd sample only 3 times (p=0.70), below the 7 required for significance.

#### 3. Discussion

Three days of heat exposure in purged, insulated growlers did not produce a detectable difference to the brewer. The short duration and oxygen-limited packaging constrain the result; longer warm storage or a less protected package could behave differently.

### [Impact Storage Temperature Has On Czech Pilsner](https://brulosophy.com/2020/07/20/impact-storage-temperature-has-on-czech-pilsner-exbeeriment-results/)

**Date:** 2020-07-20  
**Master-index p-value:** 0.02

#### 1. Purpose

Compare Czech Pilsner conditioned for 4 weeks at 34°F/1°C against the same beer conditioned at 60°F/16°C.

#### 2. Results

The brewer correctly identified the odd sample in 7 of 10 semi-blind triangle tests, meeting the significance threshold (p=0.02). The cold-conditioned beer was clearer and was perceived as crisper and cleaner.

#### 3. Discussion

Conditioning temperature produced a detectable difference in this single-brewer test. Temperature was also associated with visible clarity differences, although opaque tasting cups limited direct visual identification during the triangle tests.

### [Impact Storage Temperature Has On An English Barleywine](https://brulosophy.com/2019/04/15/impact-of-storage-temperature-on-high-og-beer-exbeeriment-results/)

**Date:** 2019-04-15  
**Master-index p-value:** 0.0009

#### 1. Purpose

Compare an English Barleywine aged for 5 months at 32°F/0°C against the same beer aged at 55°F-60°F/13°C-16°C.

#### 2. Results

Fourteen of 20 tasters identified the odd sample, exceeding the 11 required for significance (p=0.0009). Five correct tasters preferred the cool-aged beer, 6 preferred the warm-aged beer, and 3 had no preference.

#### 3. Discussion

Ageing temperature clearly changed this strong beer, but the split preference provides no basis for declaring one treatment universally better. The result is specific to five months, the tested temperatures, and this Barleywine recipe.

### [Impact Freezing Beer Has On Munich Helles](https://brulosophy.com/2018/10/22/evaluating-the-impact-freezing-beer-has-on-various-characteristics-exbeeriment-results/)

**Date:** 2018-10-22  
**Master-index p-value:** 0.29

#### 1. Purpose

Compare Munich Helles frozen for 5 days and then thawed against the same beer kept liquid.

#### 2. Results

Of 22 tasters, 9 identified the odd sample, below the 12 required for significance (p=0.29). The brewer observed an appearance difference but could not reliably distinguish the beers by aroma, flavour, or mouthfeel.

#### 3. Discussion

A single freeze-thaw event did not produce a reliably detectable sensory difference in this beer, though visual changes occurred. It does not establish safety for repeated freezing, partially filled packages, glass containers, or other beer compositions.

### [Cold-Side Oxidation: Impact Warm Storage Over Time Has On An American Pale Ale](https://brulosophy.com/2017/06/19/post-fermentation-oxidation-pt-2-evaluating-the-impact-of-age-and-storage-temperature-exbeeriment-results/)

**Date:** 2017-06-19  
**Master-index p-value:** 0.19

#### 1. Purpose

Re-evaluate an American Pale Ale packaged under low- and high-oxygen conditions after both kegs spent 100 days in warm storage.

#### 2. Results

Twenty tasters participated, and 9 correctly identified the odd sample, below the 11 required for significance (p=0.19). The panel therefore did not reliably distinguish the packaging treatments after the extended warm-ageing period.

#### 3. Discussion

The extended warm storage did not make the earlier oxygen-treatment difference reliably detectable to this panel. Since both beers were aged together and oxygen exposure remained the underlying treatment, this is evidence about oxidation development over time rather than a clean warm-versus-cold comparison.

### [Impact Storage Temperature Has On A Czech Premium Pale Lager](https://brulosophy.com/2017/02/27/the-impact-of-storage-temperature-on-beer-exbeeriment-results/)

**Date:** 2017-02-27  
**Master-index p-value:** 0.01

#### 1. Purpose

Compare a Czech Premium Pale Lager stored for one month at 35°F/2°C against the same beer held at 68°F-73°F/20°C-23°C, followed by equal cold conditioning.

#### 2. Results

Twelve of 20 tasters identified the odd sample, exceeding the 11 required for significance; the article reports p=0.013 while the index rounds to 0.01. Five correct tasters preferred the warm-stored beer, 2 preferred the cold-stored beer, 4 had no preference, and 1 reported no difference.

#### 3. Discussion

A month of warm storage produced a lasting detectable difference even after both beers subsequently spent a month cold. Preference did not align with the brewer's preference for the crisper cold-stored beer, illustrating that distinguishability and quality judgement are separate questions.

### [Impact Of Age On Pliny The Elder Double IPA](https://brulosophy.com/2016/08/11/the-impact-of-age-on-pliny-the-elder-exbeeriment-results/)

**Date:** 2016-08-11  
**Master-index p-value:** 0.14

#### 1. Purpose

Compare commercial Pliny The Elder Double IPA approximately 2 weeks old against bottles more than 2 months old.

#### 2. Results

Twenty-four tasters participated, and 11 identified the fresh sample, below the 13 required for significance (p=0.14). The panel did not reliably distinguish the two age groups.

The tested age difference was not reliably detectable in this set of bottles. Commercial batch variation, package history before acquisition, and the relatively modest age gap limit conclusions about long-term IPA ageing.

## Storage vessels and transport

**Master-index p-value:** 0.29

#### 1. Purpose

Compare American IPA stored for 2 weeks in an unpurged Crowler against the same beer served fresh from the tap.

#### 2. Results

Nine of 22 tasters identified the odd sample, below the 12 required for significance (p=0.29). The brewer described the samples as perceptibly identical.

### [Impact Of Storing Beer In A Plastic Nalgene Bottle](https://brulosophy.com/2021/12/06/exbeeriment-impact-of-storing-beer-in-a-plastic-nalgene-bottle/)

### [Impact Of Storing Beer In A Glass Growler](https://brulosophy.com/2021/05/03/impact-of-storing-beer-in-a-glass-growler-exbeeriment-results/)

#### 1. Purpose

Compare Vienna Lager stored cold for 4 days in a glass growler against beer served directly from the keg.

#### 2. Results

The brewer completed 10 semi-blind triangle tests and identified the growler sample 4 times, below the 7 required for significance (p=0.44). Carbonation, aroma, flavour, and mouthfeel were perceived as equivalent.

### [Impact Of Storing Beer In A CO2 Pressurized Growler](https://brulosophy.com/2020/10/12/impact-of-storing-beer-in-a-co2-pressurized-growler-exbeeriment-results/)

## Deliberate maturation and vessel residence

### [Impact Extended Time On Trub Has On A Kölsch](https://brulosophy.com/2023/05/08/exbeeriment-impact-extended-time-on-trub-has-on-a-kolsch/)

**Date:** 2023-05-08  
**Master-index p-value:** 0.81

#### 1. Purpose

Compare Kölsch held in direct contact with a large amount of trub for 4 weeks after fermentation against beer with minimal trub contact.

#### 2. Results

Seven of 26 tasters identified the odd sample, below the 14 required for significance (p=0.81). The brewer also considered the beers indistinguishable.

#### 3. Discussion

Four weeks of cold post-fermentation trub contact did not yield a detectable difference in this execution. This does not rule out autolysis or other effects with warmer storage, longer residence, higher pressure, unhealthy yeast, or different trub loads.

### [Impact Extended Time In Primary Fermentation Vessel Has On A Festbier](https://brulosophy.com/2019/03/18/impact-of-extended-time-in-primary-fermentation-vessel-exbeeriment-results/)

**Date:** 2019-03-18  
**Master-index p-value:** 0.012

#### 1. Purpose

Compare Festbier packaged when final gravity was reached against the same beer left in its primary vessel for an additional 3 weeks before packaging.

#### 2. Results

Thirteen of 22 tasters identified the odd sample, exceeding the 12 required for significance (p=0.012). Four correct tasters preferred the beer packaged promptly, 2 preferred the extended-primary beer, 5 had no preference, and 2 reported no difference.

#### 3. Discussion

Extended primary residence produced a detectable sensory difference, but preference was weak and mixed. The two treatments also experienced slightly different temperature histories before their shared lagering period, which complicates causal interpretation.

### [Wood Aging: Impact Contact Time With Oak Cubes Has On An American Porter](https://brulosophy.com/2019/02/04/wood-aging-the-impact-contact-time-with-oak-cubes-has-on-beer-exbeeriment-results/)

**Master-index p-value:** 0.42

#### 1. Purpose

Compare American Porter aged on the same dose of oak cubes for 2 weeks against beer aged on oak for 2 months.

#### 2. Results

Thirteen of 36 tasters identified the odd sample, below the 16 required for significance (p=0.42). The panel did not reliably distinguish the two contact times.

#### 3. Discussion

Extending oak-cube contact from 2 weeks to 2 months did not yield a reliable difference in this robust Porter. Extraction may have reached a practical plateau early, or the base beer may have masked smaller changes; other wood formats, doses, and beer strengths may differ.

### [Impact Lagering Has On A Festbier](https://brulosophy.com/2018/12/17/the-lager-effect-exbeeriment-results/)

**Date:** 2018-12-17  
**Master-index p-value:** 0.46

#### 1. Purpose

Compare Festbier given 11 additional weeks of cold lagering against the same beer cold-conditioned for only 3 weeks before serving.

#### 2. Results

Eight of 22 tasters identified the odd sample, below the 12 required for significance (p=0.46). The longer-lagered beer was visibly clearer, but blind preference among correct tasters was evenly divided.

#### 3. Discussion

A large difference in cold-conditioning duration changed appearance more clearly than flavour in this panel. Because opaque cups were used, the triangle-test p-value should not be interpreted as a test of clarity.

## Malt and hop freshness, curing and storage

### [Impact Curing Toasted Malt Has On A Märzen](https://brulosophy.com/2025/05/19/exbeeriment-impact-curing-toasted-malt-has-on-a-marzen/)

**Date:** 2025-05-19  
**Master-index p-value:** 0.02

#### 1. Purpose

Compare Märzen made with home-toasted malt cured for 5 months against beer made with the same toasted malt used without curing.

#### 2. Results

Exactly 12 of 21 tasters identified the odd sample, meeting the significance threshold (p=0.02). The beers reached the same final gravity.

#### 3. Discussion

Curing the toasted malt produced a detectable effect in this recipe. The trial does not distinguish changes caused by volatile loss, oxidation, moisture equilibration, or normal ageing during the five-month storage period.

### [Impact Using Old Cascade Hops Has On An American Pale Ale](https://brulosophy.com/2023/04/24/exbeeriment-impact-using-old-cascade-hops-has-on-an-american-pale-ale/)

**Date:** 2023-04-24  
**Master-index p-value:** 0.19

#### 1. Purpose

Compare an American Pale Ale made with Cascade hops from the 2017 crop year against one made with 2022 Cascade.

#### 2. Results

Nine of 20 tasters identified the odd sample, below the 11 required for significance (p=0.19). The batches finished 0.002 specific-gravity points apart.

#### 3. Discussion

The five-year crop-age difference did not produce a reliable panel distinction under the hops' actual storage conditions. Crop year, lot variation, alpha-acid decline, and storage history are entangled, so this is not a controlled test of age alone.

### [Hop Crop Year: 2020 vs. 2018 Triumph](https://brulosophy.com/2022/01/24/exbeeriment-hop-crop-year-2020-vs-2018-triumph/)

#### 1. Purpose

Compare an American IPA made with properly stored Triumph hops from the 2020 crop against one made with 2018 Triumph.

Two-year-old, well-stored Triumph performed similarly to the newer crop in this recipe. The result combines age with unavoidable crop-lot variation and cannot be extended to poorly sealed or warm-stored hops.

## Yeast storage, preservation and aged-yeast handling

### [Impact Fermenting With Home-Dried Yeast Has On A British Golden Ale - The Brü Club xBmt](https://brulosophy.com/2023/01/27/the-bru-club-xbmt-series-impact-fermenting-with-home-dried-yeast-has-on-a-british-golden-ale/)

**Date:** 2023-01-27  
**Master-index p-value:** 0.11

#### 1. Purpose

Compare a British Golden Ale fermented with fresh Nottingham yeast against one fermented with home-dried Nottingham stored in a refrigerator for several months.

#### 2. Results

Eleven of 23 tasters identified the odd sample, one fewer than required for significance (p=0.11). Both batches reached the same final gravity, and the brewer considered them perceptibly identical.

#### 3. Discussion

Home drying and refrigerated storage worked without a reliably detectable sensory penalty for this dry-yeast strain. Nottingham is already adapted to commercial drying, so liquid strains or less robust yeasts may not tolerate the same process.

### [Impact A Vitality Starter Of Old Yeast Has On An English Porter](https://brulosophy.com/2022/06/13/exbeeriment-impact-a-vitality-starter-of-old-yeast-has-on-an-english-porter/)

**Date:** 2022-06-13  
**Master-index p-value:** 0.41

#### 1. Purpose

Compare English Porter fermented with a vitality starter made from old packaged yeast against beer receiving the same aged yeast by direct pitch.

#### 2. Results

Nine of 24 tasters identified the odd sample, below the 13 required for significance (p=0.41). The panel did not reliably distinguish the vitality-starter and direct-pitch treatments.

#### 3. Discussion

A vitality starter did not create a detectable finished-beer difference with this aged yeast under these conditions. Fermentation kinetics and cell health can still matter even when the final sensory test is non-significant.

### [Impact Yeast Storage Temperature Has On Double IPA](https://brulosophy.com/2020/09/07/impact-yeast-storage-temperature-has-on-double-ipa-exbeeriment-results/)

**Date:** 2020-09-07  
**Master-index p-value:** 0.003

#### 1. Purpose

Compare Double IPA fermented with Imperial Yeast A18 Joystick stored cold against beer fermented with the same yeast held for 3 days at temperatures reaching 95°F/35°C.

#### 2. Results

The brewer identified the odd sample in 8 of 10 semi-blind triangle tests (p=0.003). Both beers reached the same final gravity, but the warm-stored yeast beer was perceived as fruitier and less sharply hop-forward.

#### 3. Discussion

Brief extreme heat exposure changed the beer produced by this yeast, even though attenuation was unaffected. The result came from one brewer rather than a conventional panel and does not establish that warm-stored yeast will always produce inferior beer.

### [Impact Yeast Storage Temperature Has On A British Golden Ale](https://brulosophy.com/2019/01/28/yeast-storage-temperature-cool-vs-warm-exbeeriment-results/)

**Date:** 2019-01-28  
**Master-index p-value:** 0.048

#### 1. Purpose

Compare British Golden Ale fermented with yeast refrigerated before use against beer fermented with the same yeast stored at about 68°F/20°C for 2 weeks.

#### 2. Results

Twelve of 23 tasters identified the odd sample, meeting the significance threshold (p=0.048). Eight correct tasters preferred the beer made with warm-stored yeast, 3 preferred the cold-stored yeast beer, and 1 reported no difference.

#### 3. Discussion

Room-temperature yeast storage produced a detectable effect, but the preference result did not favour the recommended cold treatment. The experiment does not negate viability concerns or justify routine warm storage, particularly for longer periods or different strains.

### [Impact Using Old Yeast Slurry Has On A Vienna Lager](https://brulosophy.com/2015/07/15/sloppy-old-slurry-starter-vs-fresh-yeast-exbeeriment-results/)

#### 1. Purpose

Compare Vienna Lager fermented with a starter made from old harvested slurry against beer fermented with fresh yeast.

#### 2. Results

The master index reports p=0.70. The comparison did not meet the conventional significance threshold, so the panel did not reliably distinguish the old-slurry starter from the fresh-yeast treatment.

## General limitations

Most exBEERiments are single brewing comparisons with modest sensory panels. Storage temperature and duration, package oxygen, fill level, closure integrity, light exposure, transport conditions, ingredient lot, crop year, microbial health, recipe composition, equipment, panel composition, statistical power, and evaluation timing can all affect the outcome. Many storage experiments also confound age with unavoidable batch, crop, or package-history differences. These summaries should therefore be used to guide brewing decisions and follow-up tests, not as universal shelf-life rules.
