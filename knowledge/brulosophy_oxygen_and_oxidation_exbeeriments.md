# Brülosophy exBEERiments: Oxygen and Oxidation

**Experiments included:** 47

**Source index:** https://brulosophy.com/projects/exbeeriments/

**Prepared:** 29 July 2026

## About this file

This catalogue indexes Brülosophy experiments in which oxygen availability, oxygen exposure, oxidation control, antioxidant treatment, or oxygen-sensitive storage was the tested variable. It is not a copy of the article text. Every experiment includes a direct link to its source article and three sections: purpose, results, and discussion.

**Scope:** Included experiments test wort aeration or oxygenation; hot-side aeration and brewing-liquor deaeration; cold-side transfers, open-vessel additions, keg purging, airlock suck-back, and oxidative packaging; sulfite, ascorbic-acid, BrewTan B, and related antioxidant treatments; and storage or portable packages where oxygen exposure or oxidation was central to the experiment. General aging, storage-temperature, fermentation-vessel, bottle-conditioning, and packaging-equipment experiments were excluded when oxygen was only a possible secondary mechanism rather than the variable being tested.

**Statistical caution:** A significant triangle test supports the conclusion that the tested samples were distinguishable to that panel under those conditions. It does not prove that oxygen caused the difference, identify the responsible oxidation compounds, or show which treatment was better. A non-significant result means that the experiment failed to detect a difference; it is not proof that oxygen uptake, dissolved oxygen, or shelf life was identical.

Several experiments changed multiple practical steps at once, such as transfer method, keg purging, and exposure to ambient air. Those entries describe the complete packaging regimes and do not claim that a single oxygen vector was isolated.

The p-value field reproduces the value shown in Brülosophy’s master index. Where an article provides a more precise value or the index omits a value, the discrepancy is stated in the results section.

## Wort aeration and oxygenation

### [Wort Aeration: Impact Oxygenation Has When Repitching Yeast In A Czech Premium Pale Lager](https://brulosophy.com/2024/10/21/exbeeriment-wort-aeration-impact-oxygenation-has-when-repitching-yeast-in-a-czech-premium-pale-lager/)

**Date:** 2024-10-21  
**Master-index p-value:** 0.40

#### 1. Purpose

Compare a Czech Premium Pale Lager made with twice-repitched yeast that received a two-minute dose of pure oxygen before pitching against an otherwise matched batch that was not oxygenated.

#### 2. Results

Twenty-one tasters participated, and 8 correctly identified the odd sample, below the 12 required for significance (p=0.40). The oxygenated beer finished at 1.019 compared with 1.017 for the non-oxygenated beer.

#### 3. Discussion

The added oxygen did not create a reliably detectable sensory difference and did not improve attenuation in this execution. This does not negate oxygen as a yeast nutrient; the untreated wort may already have contained enough oxygen for the repitched yeast.
